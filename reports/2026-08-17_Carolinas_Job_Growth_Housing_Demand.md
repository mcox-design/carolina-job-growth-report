# Carolinas Job Growth & Housing Demand Report
### Week of 2026-08-17 (Week 5)

**Coverage window:** Announcements/material milestones dated **2026-02-17 → 2026-08-17** (trailing 6 months)
**Geography:** North Carolina + South Carolina (Charlotte metro prioritized, then NC Triangle/Triad, Upstate SC, Midlands/Lowcountry)
**Purpose:** Translate high-paying job growth into a forward signal for multifamily (MF) and townhome development demand.

> **Methodology note — read first.** This is Week 5, built on the 2026-08-10 report. Unlike the prior two cycles (2026-08-03 and 2026-08-10), **all five regional research passes completed this cycle** — Charlotte metro, the NC Triangle, the NC Triad/WNC/Coastal region, Upstate SC, and SC Midlands/Lowcountry each received a full, independent recheck. This closes out a real backlog: the SC Midlands/Lowcountry region had received **zero** fresh research for two consecutive cycles, and Upstate SC had received almost none. Several primary news-site domains (wral.com, abc11.com, carolinajournal.com, hoodline.com, businessnc.com, postandcourier.com, and others) returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` on direct page-fetch attempts from this environment; findings sourced from those domains below rely on web-search-result snippets citing the same named, real URLs rather than full-page fetches, and are flagged per-item where this applies. No numbers were invented anywhere in this report; missing figures are marked **"Not disclosed"**; inferred figures are marked **"Estimated/Inferred"** with basis stated.

**Key takeaways for a MF/townhome developer:**

- **The report's #1 open question is resolved: SC Ports' Leatherman Terminal pause did take effect.** Multiple corroborating trade sources confirm the pause began Aug. 1, 2026 as announced, phasing in gradually over roughly six weeks (full wind-down around mid-September). No reopening timeline exists; SC Ports leadership says reopening depends on cargo volumes and making the terminal's ILA labor costs more competitive. This item stays Watch-tier (a wind-down, not new growth) but the two-cycle-old open question is now a confirmed fact.
- **This cycle's biggest single find is Suniva Inc.'s $350M, 564-job solar-cell plant in Laurens County, SC** — announced April 14, 2026 and missed entirely during the prior research gap. It is the third-largest investment in Laurens County history and the first U.S.-owned solar-cell facility in South Carolina. Wage is Estimated/Inferred sub-$100k blended (entry ~$23/hr, line managers up to ~$53/hr), which keeps it Watch-tier rather than Ranked, consistent with how this dataset treats other large, sub-$100k-wage manufacturing deals (Toyota Battery, Wolfspeed).
- **Scout Motors' Blythewood, SC EV plant needed a genuine correction, not just a refresh.** After five weeks without independent verification, no source this cycle could support the previously-carried ~1,400-employee figure; 600+ (as of April 2026) is the best-documented number. This is presented as a data correction, not a signal that hiring is declining — no reduction or pullback was found. Combined with newly-surfaced timeline risk on customer deliveries, the entry's score was trimmed from 86 to 83.
- **HII's Goose Creek, SC shipbuilding site is genuinely gaining momentum**, though still under the 500-job Ranked threshold: headcount has grown to roughly 475 (from a prior ~250 estimate), the company discloses production is up more than 50% under its ownership, and HII won an AUKUS-linked Australian Submarine Supplier Qualification contract in late July that should grow the site further.
- **Wolfspeed's Siler City, NC fab is a risk flag to watch closely.** Company-wide capex was cut 90% year-over-year, underutilization charges were specifically cited at Siler City, and an unverified headline suggests local layoffs. The company's Q4 FY26 earnings call is scheduled for **Aug. 19, 2026 — two days after this report** — and is the next material data point.
- **Two items were corrected as mis-scoped rather than newly discovered.** PSA Airlines' Charlotte HQ (400 jobs) was previously filed under Excluded/Noise as "pre-window," but its grand opening on **2026-03-19 falls inside every window this dataset has used since**; it is reclassified to Markets to Watch this cycle. FN America (Pickens Co., SC) is removed from Watch after a fourth consecutive cycle with no confirmed momentum, per last cycle's flag.

### 4. Blythewood / North Columbia — Scout Motors EV assembly plant (Richland, SC) — Score 83 — Updated (-3)
**What happened:** This is the first independent recheck of this entry in five weeks. Two developments cut in opposite directions. Positive: the first Traveler body was welded in the Body Shop (June 2026), and by July 2026 the Paint Shop was near completion with carriers installed and systems being tested. Negative/corrective: no source found this cycle could corroborate the previously-carried figure of "~1,400 employed companywide" — the best-supported current figure is **600+ hired as of April 20, 2026**, with 200-300 more hires planned during 2026. Separately, multiple trade outlets (CarsDirect, TFLcar, FITSNews) reported in Feb–Apr 2026 that customer vehicle deliveries may slip toward **summer 2028** (from earlier guidance), citing EREV powertrain technical issues and VW cost-cutting; Scout has publicly denied a formal delay, and AutoForecast Solutions' even-later estimates (Sept. 2028/March 2030) are also disputed by the company.
**Why it matters:** The headcount correction is treated as a data-quality fix, not a negative signal — no source indicates a hiring reduction or pullback. But combined with the newly-documented delivery-timeline risk, this justifies a real (not cosmetic) score reduction.
**Housing-demand implication:** Carried forward at reduced confidence — bifurcated demand (salaried/professional staff support higher-income product; the large hourly base drives workforce/missing-middle rental) in northern Richland/Fairfield, but headcount growth may be running slower than this dataset previously assumed.
**Source:** [Scout Motors company blog, July 2026 update](https://blog.scoutmotors.com/july-2026-scout-motors-production-center-update/)

## 7. Scoring Methodology & Excel-Ready Tables

**Composite score (0–100)** = weighted sum of six factors:

| Factor | Weight | What it rewards |
|---|---|---|
| Job count | 25 | Absolute headcount (scaled; 2,000+ ≈ full, 500 ≈ ~12) |
| Salary / income quality | 25 | Stated avg wage vs. $100k bar; inferred wages discounted |
| Employer & industry strength | 15 | Balance-sheet/brand durability, industry growth, execution certainty |
| Capital investment / project certainty | 15 | Capex size + how locked-in (incentive-backed, under construction) |
| MF / townhome demand relevance | 15 | Urban/inner-suburban fit, income tier match to Class-A / for-sale TH |
| Repeat momentum | 5 | Reinforcement of a market across reports (new deals score low; repeated/gaining-momentum deals score higher) |

**Score sensitivity to data quality:** A genuine independent recheck that cannot corroborate a previously-carried figure (Scout Motors' Blythewood headcount, this cycle) should move the score down, not just get re-stamped with the same number — carrying forward unverified data as if reconfirmed would understate real uncertainty. Conversely, a milestone that removes execution-risk (JetZero's site-prep timeline and financing LOI) moves the score up modestly even without a full construction start.
